Why we built it

Your Mac already has a speech recognizer and a ~3B-parameter language model. Apple put them there. Almost nobody uses them.

Meanwhile, AI meeting copilots charge $19.99 a month, upsell “stealth” at up to $149.99, and stream your calls to their servers to run models you’re renting. That felt backwards. So we asked one question: how far does the AI that’s already on your Mac go?

That’s why it’s free. The AI is already built into your Mac. We pay for no servers, so you pay us nothing.

Further than we expected. 2.06% word error rate. First word of an answer in 0.4 s. Notes in about five seconds. All of it in a 0.8 MB app, because the heavy parts were never ours to ship.

It isn’t a frontier model, and we won’t pretend it is. It’s great for summaries, follow-ups and “what was that number again?”, not for deep reasoning. Everyone else on the call shows up as “Them”, because Apple’s speech APIs don’t separate speakers yet. Names get misheard. Only English has been tested. We’d rather tell you now than have you find out mid-call.

      Questions

      Is it really free?

      Yes. Sotto is open source under the MIT license. There’s no account, no subscription and no server, because the AI is the one Apple already put on your Mac.

      Which Macs does it run on?

      Any Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) on macOS 26 or later, with Apple Intelligence turned on in System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri. Sotto checks at launch and tells you plainly if something’s missing.

      What permissions does it need?

      Two: the microphone, to transcribe you, and System Audio Recording Only, to transcribe everyone else. It hears audio only and never sees your screen. If the other side isn’t showing up, turn Sotto on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording.

      Can people on the call tell?

      No bot joins, so nothing appears in the participant list. Hiding the panel from screen share is opt-in, and meeting apps don’t all honour it the same way, so check your share preview before relying on it. And be a good human: in many places you need consent to record or transcribe a call.

      How good is it, honestly?

      It runs on Apple’s ~3B-parameter on-device model, which is what makes it free and private, and also sets the ceiling. It’s genuinely useful for summaries, follow-ups and “what was that number again?”, not for deep reasoning. Everyone except you is “Them”, because Apple’s speech APIs don’t separate speakers. Names get misheard; add them under Settings → Names & terms and the notes spell them right.

      Which languages?

      Your Mac’s language, if Apple’s speech recognizer supports it. Only English has been tested so far, so reports from other languages are very welcome.
